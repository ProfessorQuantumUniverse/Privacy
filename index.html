2. The core principle: no data collection

Your privacy is not just a policy; it is the core principle of these apps. This document describes the commitment to protecting your personal information by not collecting it in the first place.

None of the apps listed above collects, stores, shares or transmits any personal data or sensitive user information to the developer.

The developer operates no servers, no user accounts, no databases and no backend of any kind. There is therefore no place where personal data about you could be stored. Your use of these apps is completely anonymous to the developer. To be perfectly clear, here is a non-exhaustive list of data that is NOT collected:

  • Your name, e-mail address, phone number or any other contact information.
  • Your device's unique identifiers (Device ID, Advertising ID, IMEI, etc.).
  • Your precise or approximate location.
  • Your contacts, calendars, messages or call logs.
  • Your photos, videos, audio or any files stored on your device.
  • The list of apps installed on your device, your clipboard or your search and scan history.
  • Usage analytics, behavioural profiles, crash reports or telemetry of any kind.
  • Payment or financial information – the apps contain no purchases and no subscriptions.

This matches the "Data safety" declaration of every listed app on Google Play: no data collected, no data shared with third parties.

3. Data that stays on your device

Some apps need to remember things in order to work at all – for example your reminders in reReminder, your search history in Gsearch14 or your encrypted VirusTotal API key and saved reports in Veto. This information is stored exclusively in the app's private storage area on your own device, using standard Android mechanisms. It is:

  • never transmitted to the developer;
  • never transmitted to any third party;
  • not accessible to other apps on your device;
  • deleted completely when you clear the app's data or uninstall the app.

Under data protection law this locally stored content is not "processed" by the developer, because the developer never has access to it. If you have enabled Android's own device backup, Android may include app data in your personal backup; this is controlled by you and your backup provider, not by the developer. Veto explicitly excludes your API key from any backup.

4. Internet connections and third parties

Some apps must contact an external service to fulfil their core function – for example to retrieve live wait times, to display search results or to check a file with VirusTotal. In that case no data is sent to the developer, but a direct connection between your device and that external service takes place. As with any request on the internet, the operator of that service necessarily receives technical connection data such as your IP address, the time of the request and your user agent, and, where applicable, the content of your request (for example a search term you typed). This data is processed by that provider under its own privacy policy, over which the developer has no control.

The concrete services used by each app are listed in section 6 and on the app-specific pages. Beyond those, the apps contain:

  • no advertising networks and no ads;
  • no analytics or tracking SDKs (no Firebase Analytics, no Google Analytics, no Crashlytics, no attribution SDKs);
  • no crash reporters and no telemetry – the apps never „phone home“;
  • no social media SDKs;
  • no in-app purchases and no payment processing.

The distribution platform itself (Google Play) collects data about downloads and updates independently of these apps. That processing is governed by the Google Privacy Policy.

5. Permissions

An app only requests the Android permissions it needs for its own functionality. A permission is never used to gather information for the developer. Any data accessed through a permission remains exclusively on your device and is processed locally and only for the immediate purpose of the feature you are using. The permissions actually used are listed per app in section 6.

reReminder – com.olaf.rereminder

Purpose: a reminder app that repeatedly notifies you about healthy habits (drinking water, taking breaks, medication, movement).

Data stored on your device: the reminders you create (title, interval, schedule) and your app settings.

Data sent anywhere: none. The app works 100 % offline and does not even request the internet permission, so it is technically unable to send data anywhere.

Gsearch14 – Search without AI – com.olafsapp.gsearch14

Purpose: an independent third-party utility that modifies your search query so that web search results are shown as a plain list of links instead of AI-generated summaries, and opens them in an in-app browser.

Data stored on your device: your app settings (for example search engine and theme), your search history and bookmarks, and the cookies and cache of the in-app browser. This never leaves your device (cookies only go back to the website that set them) and is removed when you clear the app data or uninstall the app. Incognito mode and cookie blocking are available in the settings.

Data sent to third parties: the search term you enter is sent to the search engine you selected (Google Search, Marginalia Search or Wikipedia) in order to return results, exactly as it would be in any browser. If search suggestions are switched on, the text typed so far is sent to that engine's suggestion service (for Marginalia: DuckDuckGo). When you open a result, your device contacts the website you selected. Those providers receive your IP address and the usual technical request data and process it under their own privacy policies – for example the Google Privacy Policy. Nothing is sent to the developer.

Note: Gsearch14 is an independent utility and is not affiliated with, endorsed by or sponsored by Google LLC.

Permissions: internet access and network state.

Phantaland Queue Times – com.quantum_prof.phantalandwaittimes

Purpose: an unofficial app that shows current wait times for the attractions of the Phantasialand theme park.

Data stored on your device: your app settings (for example filters and sort order), the wait-time alerts you set and, where applicable, a temporary cache of the wait-time data.

Veto – com.quantum_prof.vtscansuite

Purpose: an open source VirusTotal client. You can submit a file, a link or one of your installed apps to VirusTotal and read the analysis report. You use your own free VirusTotal API key.

Data stored on your device: your VirusTotal API key (encrypted with an AES-256-GCM key from the Android Keystore and excluded from backups), the reports you choose to save and your settings. Files you pick are copied temporarily to the app's cache for hashing and deleted after the scan.

Data sent to third parties: only when you start a scan, and only to the VirusTotal API (a service of Google), directly over HTTPS with your own API key: for files and apps first only the SHA-256 hash and, if VirusTotal does not know the file yet, the file itself (for apps: the APK); for links the URL. VirusTotal processes this under its own privacy policy and terms; according to those terms, submitted files and URLs may be shared with the security community – please do not upload files containing personal or confidential information. Nothing is sent to the developer.

Note: Veto is an independent client and is not affiliated with, endorsed by or sponsored by VirusTotal or Google LLC.

Permissions: internet access, query all packages (to list your installed apps locally so you can pick one – the list never leaves your device), notifications and a foreground service (scan progress and result), vibration (haptic feedback). The clipboard is only checked locally for a link to offer a „scan this link“ shortcut.

7. Security

The best protection for your data is not to have it. All apps follow the same security principles:

  • No developer server. There is no backend, no database and no account system that could be hacked, leak data or be forced to hand over data – because none exists.
  • Direct, encrypted connections. Where an app needs the internet, it talks directly to the service you use (search engine, wait-time API, VirusTotal) – never via the developer – and the app's own requests use HTTPS.
  • Offline where possible. reReminder does not even have the internet permission; its data technically cannot leave your phone.
  • Secrets are encrypted. Veto stores your VirusTotal API key encrypted with an AES-256-GCM key from the Android Keystore and excludes it from backups and device transfers.
  • Android app sandbox. Everything an app stores lives in its private storage area, which other apps cannot access.
  • Least privilege. Each app requests only the permissions it needs for its own features (listed in section 6). None of them has access to your location, contacts, camera or microphone.
  • No third-party SDKs that collect data. The apps are built only from open source libraries – no Google Play Services, no Firebase, no ad or analytics SDKs.
  • Public, reviewable code. Every change is visible in the public Git history and checked by automated builds (GitHub Actions).

Found a security issue? Please report it confidentially by e-mail to makerlab.fffm@gmail.com. Reports are taken seriously and fixed as quickly as possible.

9. Your rights

Under the GDPR you have the right of access, rectification, erasure, restriction of processing, data portability and objection, as well as the right to lodge a complaint with a supervisory authority. Because the developer never receives or stores any personal data about you, there is no data set that could be disclosed, corrected or deleted. You can exercise the practical equivalent of erasure at any time by clearing the app's data or uninstalling the app: everything the app has stored is then gone.

If you are a resident of California, the same applies to your rights under the CCPA/CPRA. No personal information is collected, sold or shared.
